# Annotated References

Status key:

- **Verified**: citation confirmed against Crossref or the publisher/PMC page.
- **Summary only**: the paper exists, but the findings below come from a secondary summary. Read the full text before quoting numbers.

Last updated: 2026-10-15.

## Surveillance and mortality

**O'Dare K, Winch A, Beidel DC, Jiang J, Rice J, Carlson-Moore B, McCoy SA.** Suicide Deaths among Florida Fire Service Personnel, January 1, 1999 through March 1, 2026. UCF RESTORES, June 2026.
[PDF](https://ucfrestores.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/61/2026/06/Florida_Fire_Service_Suicide_Data_Final-June2026.pdf) · copy in `papers/` · **Verified (read in full)**
Linked vital statistics, EMS licensure, and firefighter certification records. n=307 deaths. 66.4% firearm, 30.5% veterans, 37.1% inactive licensure, 96.1% male. Counts only, no rates, so it cannot show relative risk.

**Singh et al.** Suicide mortality in United States urban career firefighters and emergency medical service providers. *Preventive Medicine* 2026;209:108608. doi:[10.1016/j.ypmed.2026.108608](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2026.108608) · **Verified (citation)**; findings **Summary only**
Cohort of career FDNY and non-FDNY responders. Reported suicide SMRs below US and NYC adult comparisons. Also linked PTSD and depressive symptoms to higher suicide risk. Conflicts with the NOMS analysis below, so useful for framing the denominator problem. Also see the related abstract in *Epidemiology* 2026;37, doi:[10.1097/01.ede.0001281952.33099.43](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.ede.0001281952.33099.43).

**Vigil NH, Beger S, Gochenour KS, Frazier WH, Vadeboncoeur TF, Bobrow BJ.** Suicide Among the Emergency Medical Systems Occupation in the United States. *West J Emerg Med* 2021;22(2):326-332. doi:[10.5811/westjem.2020.10.48742](https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2020.10.48742) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7972356/) · **Verified**
National Occupational Mortality Surveillance data, 26 states. Firefighters had a higher proportion of deaths by suicide than the general working population, highest at ages 65-90. EMTs elevated overall but not significantly, significant at ages 18-64.

**Heyman M, Dill J, Douglas R.** The Ruderman White Paper on Mental Health and Suicide of First Responders. Ruderman Family Foundation, April 2018. [Page](https://rudermanfoundation.org/white_papers/police-officers-and-firefighters-are-more-likely-to-die-by-suicide-than-in-line-of-duty/) · **Verified (page); authors per secondary coverage**
At least 103 firefighter suicides in 2017 vs 93 line-of-duty deaths. Reported rate 18 per 100,000 vs 13 for the general population. Relies on voluntary reporting (the Firefighter Behavioral Health Alliance estimates about 40% of firefighter suicides are reported), so counts are likely undercounts.

**Ruderman Family Foundation.** 2022 update to the first responder suicide white paper (Bar Nissim HS, Folino C, Douglas R, Dill J, Johnson O). Released June 13, 2022. Same page as above · **Summary only**
Reported that first responder suicides showed moderate to no decrease during COVID-19 while general-population suicides fell. Firearms were the most common method for firefighters and EMS.

## Prevalence of suicidal thoughts and behaviors

**Tahernejad et al.** Prevalence of suicidal ideation, suicide plan, and suicide attempt among firefighters: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Public Health in Practice* 2026;11:100709. doi:[10.1016/j.puhip.2025.100709](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.puhip.2025.100709) · **Verified (citation)**; pooled estimates not yet read
The most recent pooled estimates. Read first when planning prevalence measures.

**Stanley IH, Hom MA, Joiner TE.** A systematic review of suicidal thoughts and behaviors among police officers, firefighters, EMTs, and paramedics. *Clin Psychol Rev* 2016;44:25-44. doi:[10.1016/j.cpr.2015.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2015.12.002) · **Verified (citation)**
Foundational review of the first responder literature.

**Stanley IH, Hom MA, Hagan CR, Joiner TE.** Career prevalence and correlates of suicidal thoughts and behaviors among firefighters. *J Affect Disord* 2015;187:163-171. doi:[10.1016/j.jad.2015.08.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2015.08.007) · **Verified (citation)**; figures **Summary only**
National web survey of 1,027 current and retired firefighters. Reported career prevalence of ideation about 46.8%, plans 19.2%, attempts 15.5%. Convenience sample, so not generalizable.

**Stanley IH, et al.** Suicidal thoughts and behaviors among women firefighters: An examination of associated features and comparison with men. *J Affect Disord* 2017;221:107-114. doi:[10.1016/j.jad.2017.06.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.06.016) · **Verified (citation)**
Relevant because women are 3.9% of deaths in the Florida data.

**Martin CE, Tran JK, Buser SJ.** Correlates of suicidality in firefighter/EMS personnel. *J Affect Disord* 2017;208:177-183. doi:[10.1016/j.jad.2016.08.078](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2016.08.078) · **Verified (citation)**; findings **Summary only**
Large urban department (N about 3,000). Depression and PTSD symptom severity were the main correlates of lifetime ideation and attempts.

**Boffa JW, et al.** PTSD symptoms and suicidal thoughts and behaviors among firefighters. *J Psychiatr Res* 2017;84:277-283. doi:[10.1016/j.jpsychires.2016.10.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2016.10.014) · **Verified (citation)**

**Hom MA, et al.** Mental health service use among firefighters with suicidal thoughts and behaviors. *Psychiatr Serv* 2016;67:688-691. doi:[10.1176/appi.ps.201500177](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ps.201500177) · **Verified (citation)**
Among 483 firefighters with a history of suicidal thoughts or behaviors, those with a past attempt were more likely to have used medication and counseling than those with ideation only.

**Park et al.** Prevalence and correlates of suicidal ideation in Korean firefighters: a nationwide study. *BMC Psychiatry* 2019. doi:[10.1186/s12888-019-2388-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-019-2388-9) · [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6937629/) · **Summary only**
N=45,698. Past-year ideation 10.66%. Traumatic experiences, occupational stress, and officer duty associated with ideation beyond PTSD and depression. Non-US, useful for comparison.

## Ideas for next searches
